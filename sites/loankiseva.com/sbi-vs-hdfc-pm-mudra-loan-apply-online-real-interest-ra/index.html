1. Core Mechanics, Statutory Architecture & 2026 Regulatory Rules

The PM Mudra scheme operates under RBI’s MUDRA Act, 2006, and is implemented through scheduled commercial banks, NBFCs, and micro‑finance institutions. SBI and HDFC are classified as Category‑I and Category‑II lending institutions respectively, each subject to the Reserve Bank’s Master Direction on Digital Lending (MDDL) issued in March 2024. These directions mandate end‑to‑end encryption, real‑time consent logs, and a 48‑hour cooling‑off period for all online loan contracts, ensuring that digital applications for SBI versus HDFC PM Mudra loans adhere to uniform data‑protection standards.

Consumer rights are enshrined in the Fair Practices Code (FPC) and the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Applicants must receive a loan estimate within 24 hours of submission, a detailed cost‑breakdown before disbursement, and a clear right to exit the loan without penalty during the first 30 days. The RBI’s Supervisory Department conducts quarterly audits of digital loan origination systems, flagging any deviation from mandatory disclosure of APR, total fees, and prepayment clauses.

Real‑world benchmarks guide borrowers: the baseline interest spread for SBI is 3.35 % over MCLR, while HDFC’s spread is 3.40 % over its base rate. The statutory reserve requirement for Mudra loans is 0.5 % of the sanctioned amount, and the risk‑weighting for micro‑enterprises remains at 75 % under Basel‑III norms. 2. Borrower & Consumer Eligibility Criteria & Bureau Benchmarks

Eligibility for SBI PM Mudra loans hinges on a CIBIL score of 600‑750, a documented enterprise with at least 12 months of operation, and an annual turnover of ₹2 lakh to ₹10 lakh for street‑vendor categories, or ₹5 lakh to ₹15 lakh for manufacturing units. HDFC mirrors these parameters but extends the upper turnover limit to ₹20 lakh for service‑based enterprises, reflecting its broader risk appetite for higher‑value micro‑projects.

Financial assessment ratios focus on debt‑service coverage. Lenders calculate a debt‑to‑income ratio not exceeding 0.40, meaning monthly loan instalments must stay below 40 % of the applicant’s net monthly income. Cash‑flow evaluations examine three months of bank statements, looking for consistent positive balances and a minimum margin of ₹5,000 after fixed obligations. Existing liabilities such as credit‑card dues or personal loans are factored into the net‑income calculation, potentially reducing the sanction amount.

Statutory documentation includes KYC (Aadhaar, PAN), proof of business (shop licence or Udyam registration), and a projected cash‑flow statement. Both SBI and HDFC employ e‑verification with the NPCI’s Aadhaar‑linked authentication service, eliminating physical visits for most applicants. The verification timeline averages 6‑8 hours for SBI and 4‑6 hours for HDFC, thanks to AI‑driven document OCR and cross‑checking against the CIBIL bureau.
